# Material comparison

Keep the unmodified PETG + TPU 85A/90A finger as the geometric and grasping control. Do not change the insert's infill and its cover material in the same experiment.

Values checked 2026-10-10. Shore 00 and Shore A numbers are different scales. Hardness is not a measured friction coefficient, modulus, or lifetime.

## Controlled specimen matrix

Begin with three independent 30 × 14 × 0.40 mm specimens of C1–C4. Use the same tool finish, test substrate, contact area, and cure age. Add 0.80 and 1.20 mm thicknesses for the two best candidates. Print C5 with an explicitly recorded grade, layer height, wall strategy, raster direction, and measured thickness; it is not process-identical to a cast sample.

Use the three-cavity molds supplied in the release. Do not call three cavities from one pour three independent mixing batches. Record batch and specimen separately. Distribute the repeats across at least two mixes.

Measure thickness at five locations, cured mass, visible voids, tack, bond failure, and recovery after compression. For friction, use the same clean glass, metal, and plastic counterfaces at a documented normal load, pull direction, speed, and contact area. Record onset force and sliding force separately. Do not convert a tilt angle or pull force to friction without recording the corresponding load and setup.

## Casting procedure

Read the linked manufacturer's bulletin and SDS for the material being used. For these platinum silicones, use compatible gloves and tooling; latex and some contaminants can inhibit cure. Perform a small cure-compatibility test on the actual printed tool and sealer before casting all parts. Mix the weighed components thoroughly, degas when equipment permits, and avoid introducing bubbles into the narrow cavities.

Use small batches, a level tool, and the documented room-temperature cure. A demold time is not proof that different chemistries have the same final properties. For the initial comparison, test at a consistent 48-hour age after mixing and record temperature. Do not apply optional hot post-cures to printed tooling without checking the tooling's temperature capability.

VytaFlex is moisture-sensitive and uses its own release-agent procedure. Keep it as a separate batch with separate mixing tools. Follow its specific SDS and ventilation requirements rather than treating it as interchangeable with silicone.

## Decide with measurements

Advance a material only when it gives acceptable contact sensitivity, force drift, friction on relevant objects, intact edges, and repeatable replacement. Softness alone is not a selection rule. Repeat the force calibration after changing cover material, cover thickness, saddle hardness, or adhesive.
