# F1 development checkpoint

F1 is not a fabrication release. Its current allocation has 64 proposed
pixels: eight per nail prong and twelve per TPU patch. The native contact
locations use the retained R1 nail and saddle surfaces. Copper routing,
complete layer stacks, tail routes, electronics and physical behavior remain
to be completed.

The first layout is generated by `layout_f1_cells.py`. It follows the tapered
nail outlines; a rectangular grid would allocate some pixels outside the
leading ends. The smallest proposed overlap is 5.127 mm² before adding feeds,
vias, coverlay constraints or registration allowances.

## Electrical findings

`screen_f1_matrix.py` models the four row drivers, six local row branches and
16 column nodes. Each patch has its own trace resistance. The selected column
has a finite path to an ideal amplifier reference; other columns float.
Each position is normalized to its modeled one-contact response. This is
an electrical interaction study, not a physical force calibration.

The first path assumptions were 0.8 Ω at a row driver, 1.5 Ω on each local row
branch and 5.5 Ω on the selected column. With inactive rows clamped to bias,
the worst modeled interaction is 13.27%, 1.61% and 0.164% for pressed
resistances of 100 Ω, 1 kΩ and 10 kΩ, respectively. Floating inactive rows
perform substantially worse.

A buffered/sensed candidate reduced the path targets to 0.05 Ω, 0.25 Ω and
0.35 Ω. Its 100 Ω case still failed the provisional 1% electrical criterion.
Changing the row-driver target to 0.02 Ω did not resolve that limiting case:
the largest error remains the all-pressed pattern, dominated by column path
resistance. All evaluated configurations are retained in timestamped runs.

The current development budget is 0.02 Ω at the buffered row distribution
land, 0.25 Ω per row branch and 0.25 Ω for the remaining column path. Meeting
these values requires actual circuit regulation, connector specifications
and routed-conductor checks. A favorable result with those targets does not
prove that a board or flex circuit satisfies them.

With those targets and ideal amplifiers, the largest modeled interaction is
0.741%, 0.0749% and 0.00746% at 100 Ω, 1 kΩ and 10 kΩ. The model passes its
1% work criterion. Its uniform-array result also matches the independent
analytic relation `G = 1 / (R + Rdriver + Rbranch + rows × Rcolumn)`.
Offsets, leakage, switching and material effects remain outside this result.

The candidate circuit puts a unity-gain buffer after each row-selection
switch. Separate current and high-impedance sense multiplexers would close
the selected-column feedback loop at its connector. This can remove the
current multiplexer resistance from the controlled column potential, while
the FPC resistance remains. A parked feedback path, correct switching order,
loop stability, leakage, amplifier offsets and ADC range must be checked.

The OPA2320 was an early amplifier candidate. The OPAx392 family is also being
reviewed because lower offset matters when several cells conduct together.
Neither a component choice nor a complete analog circuit has been frozen.
[OPAx392 manufacturer reference](https://www.ti.com/product/OPA2392)

## Flex construction direction

The current routing targets are in `designs/f1_flex_targets.json`. Use two
copper layers for the **nail row electrodes**, with their feeds on the back.
The rigid nail supports those static vias. This retains leading contact
width. Use one copper layer for the other ten flex parts so the TPU patches
and tails can flex without plated vias in their moving regions.

The proposed material system uses 25 µm PI and 35 µm rolled-annealed copper.
The cited fabricator lists 0.11 mm single-layer covered flex, 0.20 mm for the
corresponding double-layer option, and reduced thickness where copper or
coverlay is absent. It also lists ±20% trace-width tolerance, which must
enter the resistance calculation. The proposed 31.5 µm minimum copper is
an additional purchasing/inspection requirement; it is not a published
supplier guarantee. [Fabrication capability reference](https://jlcpcb.com/capabilities/flex-pcb-capabilities/)

Use distinct row/column masks and explicit assembly-side transforms. The
upper conductor faces the film, and the lower conductor faces outward from
the support. Nail-plane and TPU-unroll coordinates have different handedness;
do not mirror every drawing using the same shortcut.

Both exposed conductor planes and their neutral surfaces need their own
developed lengths on a curved TPU patch. Keep adhesive out of active
electrode/film contacts. Final areas and layer offsets must be derived from
the routed artwork rather than the initial pixel-allocation plot.

The nominal saddle radii range down to 3.494 mm. Fabricator bend guidelines
are starting points for a circuit, not lifetime guarantees for this layered
tactile assembly. Measure loaded curvature, relative layer slip, bending
response and fatigue on the complete specimen.

## Next concrete work

1. Complete component limits, reference/gain circuitry and the switched-feedback stability review.
2. Route the flex patterns, calculate actual path resistances with tolerances, and verify that each pixel and feed fits.
3. Design finished FPC contact thickness, mounting/sealing tools, tail routes and strain relief.
4. Supply checked PCB/CAD files, firmware, raw acquisition, BOM, assembly instructions and actual-geometry visuals.
5. Build and test the complete finger against R1 and the common campaign. Electrical resistance cases cannot establish a 20 N force rating.

## Checked flat-artwork milestone

Twelve flat routing witnesses now exist: one row and one column foil for each
of six patches. Both nail row foils use backside feeds; the other ten foils
currently have one copper layer. The routed overlap map contains 64 distinct
regions. Its smallest copper overlap is 5.1199 mm² after removing the plated
drill holes.

All twelve pass the independent copper, outline and connectivity checks in
KiCad 10.0.7. The narrowest modeled copper clearance is 0.1889 mm, against
the 0.15 mm rule. The source-derived nail outline contained submicron edges;
the new flex outline simplifies these within a measured 0.003 mm bound.
The original source finger is unchanged.

The sensing and cable contacts intentionally share coverlay openings, with
no solder paste on the flex. `check_f1_flex.py` retains both unsuppressed
reports and reports with the scoped `bridged_mask` allowance for the
unsoldered `FOIL` footprint. It permits no other ignored rule in that block
and requires zero copper/connectivity findings. A separate probe confirms
that a 5 mm clearance sentinel and an injected copper short are still
detected with the coverlay allowance enabled.

The development exports omit KiCad's generic even-layer/FR4 Gerber job file.
Raw exports remain preserved; the selected files carry the actual proposed
polyimide stack and a development-only notice. These exports are **not the
completed F1 finger package**.

Reproduce this stage with the prepared CAD/KiCad tools:

```sh
build/engineering/tools/cad-venv/bin/python engineering/scripts/develop_f1_artwork.py
/usr/bin/python3 engineering/scripts/check_f1_flex.py ACTUAL_ARTWORK_RUN --export
build/engineering/tools/cad-venv/bin/python engineering/scripts/summarize_f1_artwork.py ACTUAL_ARTWORK_RUN
```

Use the actual timestamped directory printed by the first command. Generated
results and failed attempts remain under
`build/engineering/prototypes/f1/artwork_runs/`.

## Installed routing direction

The flat tails demonstrate electrical routing. They have not been fitted to
the gripper and must not be laid across the following contact patches.
`designs/f1_integration_development.json` records the current integration
direction: a removable connector board on the fixed finger and a separate
analog/ADC board at the wrist/base, with separate current/sense paths.

The connector-board position, folded tail paths, mating planes, mechanical
carrier and analog implementation are candidates awaiting checks. This
keeps the full installed-finger requirement open. Neither the successful
flat-artwork checks nor the contact-area map qualifies bending, contact
reliability, 20 N force performance or production yield.
