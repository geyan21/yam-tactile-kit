# Calibration exercise and measured-data protocol

The included CSV is synthetic. It demonstrates how to fit and evaluate a two-channel force model; its numerical accuracy says nothing about a fabricated sensor.

## Mathematical model

For the exercise, normal force is modeled as:

```text
F_normal = a × raw_1 + b × raw_2 + offset
```

Training data estimates the three coefficients. Raw features are centered and scaled before solving the least-squares system. Rank-deficient data is rejected. Evaluation uses only rows marked `test`; those rows never enter the fit.

This simple model does not compensate temperature, creep, dynamic acceleration, nonlinear response, or cross-axis loads. A real sensor may need a more appropriate model.

## CSV contract

The columns must be exactly:

```text
dataset_kind,sensor_id,sample_id,partition,contact_z_mm,raw_1,raw_2,reference_force_n
```

`dataset_kind` is `synthetic` or `measured`. A file contains one sensor identity and one dataset kind. Sample IDs must be unique. `partition` is `train` or `test`. Length is in millimeters and reference normal force is in newtons. Raw channels use the consistent native units recorded for that sensor.

At least six training and three held-out samples are required by the exercise. Training must contain at least two contact positions. Those numerical minimums are not a sufficient physical calibration campaign.

The supplied data is generated by the saved `scripts/make_synthetic_example.py` with a fixed seed. Its training contacts are at 30, 45, and 60 mm; its held-out contact is at 55 mm. A changing applied end moment helps identify the two channels independently.

## Measured-data collection

Use a reference force instrument with documented uncertainty below the acceptance band. Record sensor/ADC identity, gain settings, temperature, finger revision, tare procedure, load position, direction, and fixture alignment in a separate session record.

Collect unloaded readings and increasing/decreasing force ramps at the nose, center, and edges. Include varied lever arms. Keep complete load cycles or positions held out, rather than randomly splitting nearly identical adjacent samples.

Characterize pure shear and torsion, loading/unloading hysteresis, ten-minute holds, recovery, temperature changes, finger orientation, and replacement. Root sensing also sees finger weight and inertia; compensate or bound them before interpreting robot-motion data as object contact.

Export the normal-force calibration subset into the documented CSV. Preserve all original recordings independently.

## Run and inspect

```sh
/usr/bin/python3 scripts/calibrate.py \
  --input examples/calibration.synthetic.csv \
  --output build/calibration-report.json
```

Inspect the coefficients, training/held-out sample counts and positions, RMSE, maximum absolute error, and individual predictions. The report always sets `hardware_qualified` to `false`: a fitted regression does not perform the physical validation campaign.

For measured data, also inspect residuals against force, contact position, temperature, time, direction, and sensor identity. Do not turn the report's small aggregate error into a general claim about every operating condition.

The website plots reference versus predicted held-out force and prominently identifies the dataset kind. Rebuild with `--calibration your-file.csv` to display another report.

## Calibration storage and replacement

Store the calibration with the adapter serial, native channel units, electrical gain/range, finger revision, force frame, temperature range, date, and the source-data hash. The exercise report is a starting format, not a complete production calibration certificate.

After a finger swap, verify seating, zero, and reference load. After replacing an array, use its own taxel map and calibration. Avoid automatic zeroing while the finger is holding a sustained load.
