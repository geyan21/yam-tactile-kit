Need ENPIRE's thin nail and cradle? ENPIRE-T fits the same socket and carries {{e_taxels}} taxels, and ENPIRE-T Tip keeps the stock cradle and senses at the nail.

A flat bay instead of a curved fingertip

Unfolding the ENPIRE STL into a custom FPC kept failing. Its contact faces are not the problem, since the nail face is a plane and the cradle a straight extrusion. The detail on them is: 1 mm teeth far too tight for polyimide to follow, a lattice that flexes, and a narrow nail split by a groove.

This design moves the irregular shape into the printed core and the skin, and gives the sensor one flat face. The pad is a plain rectangle, and every trace leaves through the base, away from the narrow tip. ENPIRE-T takes the other route and keeps ENPIRE's shape.

The cartridge keeps the stock tool point at z = {{tcp_z}} mm, so existing configs and IK targets still work.

ENPIRE-T: the ENPIRE finger with {{e_taxels}} taxels

For tasks that need ENPIRE's thin grooved nail and its serrated cradle. ENPIRE-T is that finger rebuilt as a cartridge for the same socket, so one socket takes any of these fingers.

A flat laminate can follow both of ENPIRE's contact faces exactly, so ENPIRE-T keeps those faces and changes what sits on them.

The cradle becomes part of one rigid core, with a smooth seat {{e_seat_set}} mm under the original surface. A strip of {{e_strip}} taxels lies on the seat, under a {{e_skin_t}} mm molded silicone skin that carries ENPIRE's teeth. The nail keeps its outside and gets {{e_nail}} taxels around its groove, in a {{e_pocket}} mm pocket under a {{e_film_t}} mm film, flush with the original face.

One laminate runs from the flange down the cradle and onto the nail. Everything it touches unrolls exactly, so each taxel's position comes from the CAD and a finger needs no position calibration, only the usual zeroing.

Limits, and what isn't verified yet

The nail faces are tilted {{e_tilt}}° so the tips meet first, as on stock ENPIRE. A rigid flat part held at the tips therefore loads only the bare tip: in MuJoCo, a flat plate pinched there puts {{e_flat_n}} N on the nail taxels. Round and soft objects, and anything held further up the nail, do reach them. The {{e_gap_band}} mm between the last strip row and the first nail row has no taxels either.

Nothing has been printed, laminated or loaded. The finger PCB's readout still needs its layout, in {{e_free_mm2}} mm² of free board. Before a batch, test-print one core, trial-fit a skin on it, dry-fit both tails with paper cut-outs, and laminate on the core rather than flat. On the first laminate, look for wrinkling on the arch, where a few strip rows sit on bends of {{e_bend_r}} mm radius.

ENPIRE-T Tip: the stock cradle and a sensing nail

When a task needs touch only at the nail, ENPIRE-T Tip senses there and keeps ENPIRE's compliant TPU cradle, which you print from the original file. It fits the same socket.

The nail is ENPIRE-T's: a flush pocket under a {{t_film_t}} mm film, here with {{t_taxels}} taxels at {{t_pitch}} mm around the groove. The frame is ENPIRE's, with a recess for the laminate along the back spine, the open back behind the insert's top corner closed, and the flange. On the contact side the one other change is at the nail root: over the laminate's width its corner is rounded off and the film-covered laminate wraps round it, up to {{t_edge_deep}} mm inside the stock surface and nowhere outside it, and the slit beside the insert is wider there.

The laminate's two sheets wrap round the nail root together and part at the foot of the slit beside the insert. From there their tails run side by side, one sheet thick, in a {{t_recess}} mm recess along the spine, at least {{t_gap_tpu}} mm clear of the insert, and rise into the flange at the back of the cavity. The finger PCB keeps ENPIRE-T's outline and pogo targets, with smaller connectors at the back of the cavity.

The taxels all sit on the flat nail, so their positions come straight from the CAD, and every surface on the route unrolls exactly, so the laminate fits without stretching.

    How it goes together

    1. The socket replaces the stock tip on the carriage, using the same heat-set inserts and screws, and carries a small pogo-pin board. It stays on the robot.
    2. The sensor pad, an FPC, Velostat and FPC laminate, sits flush in the cartridge's flat bay. Its two tails run up a groove and into the finger PCB.
    3. The finger PCB digitises all {{taxels}} taxels inside the cartridge and talks USB to the host over five pogo pins.
    4. The TPU skin presses onto the face. Ribs on its side walls snap into grooves, so payload shear can't walk it off the tip.
    5. To dock, push the cartridge straight up onto two dowels and hand-tighten two thumbscrews. Swapping a finger needs no tools.

    {{taxels}} taxels, digitised inside the finger

    Rows and columns are copper electrodes on two FPCs with Velostat between them; each crossing is a taxel. The layout follows the FlexiTac V2 process.

    Pogo pins

    The ground pins are longer, so ground connects first when you dock a cartridge with the robot powered.

    Frame format

    AA 55verflagsseqt_us{{taxels}} × u16 pressureCRC

    268 bytes per frame, little endian, row-major with row 0 at the tip. The checksum is CRC-16/CCITT-FALSE. Tail pins 9–16 carry rows 15 down to 8, and host/tactile_tools.py puts them back in order.

    Checked against the robot's own geometry

    Every number here is measured from the exported parts and i2rt's YAM meshes by cad/check_fit.py and cad/measure.py.

    • At least {{clear_opp}} mm of clearance to the opposite carriage, and {{clear_housing}} mm to the housing, across the full 0–47.5 mm stroke.
    • Walls of at least {{wall_pad}} mm behind the pad, and {{wall_min_hole}}–{{wall_max_hole}} mm around every insert and screw hole.
    • The only overlaps are intended ones: the skins squeeze {{squeeze}} mm each at full close, the pogo pins compress 0.7 mm, and the dowels press-fit.
    • In MuJoCo, a 24 mm block held at a {{sim_grip}} N grip reads {{sim_left}} N on each finger across {{sim_taxels}} taxels.
    • An independent review of v0.1 found the socket scraping the opposite carriage below about 23 mm of opening. v{{version}} drops that wing by {{relief}} mm, as the stock tip does.

    Not verified yet

    Nothing has been printed, fitted to a real gripper, or loaded. Pad sensitivity, drift, skin wear and the finger PCB firmware are still open.

    Before you print a batch

    1. Confirm that the stock tip screws go in from above, through the carriage, into the tip's heat-set inserts. The socket copies that arrangement.
    2. Measure a laminated pad. It should sit flush in the {{pad_t}} mm pocket, or at most 0.05 mm proud.
    3. Check the ZIF keep-outs and the FPC entry height against the connectors you buy.
    4. Print one socket and one cartridge. Check the dowel fit, the skin's snap, and the {{squeeze}} mm squeeze at full close.

    Printing

    Print the cartridge with the docking face on the bed and the finger pointing up, with no supports: only the PCB seat bridges. Use 0.2 mm layers, four or five walls and at least 40% infill, in PETG or PA-CF. The socket prints on its carriage face, and the skin on its contact face in TPU 85A.

    Keep the gripper force limit at about 80 N or below.
